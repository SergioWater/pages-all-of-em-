Emergency Service

Emergency Tree Service

A failed limb over a roof, a tree down across a driveway, a hazardous lean after a storm — these need prompt, careful attention. Here's how to handle the situation and how we help.

Storms, saturated soil, and wind can bring down limbs or whole trees with little warning. When that happens, the priority is safety first, then a controlled response to prevent further damage.

Emergency response availability: confirm — verify hours and after-hours response before publishing. We do not advertise guaranteed timeframes we can't promise.

If a tree has just failed — stay safe first

  • Keep people and pets well clear of the tree and any limbs under tension
  • Never touch a tree or limb that is on or near a power line — treat all lines as live and call your utility and 911 as appropriate
  • Don't attempt to cut limbs under load yourself; spring-loaded wood is dangerous
  • Document damage with photos for your insurance once it is safe to do so

How we respond

Rapid assessment

We evaluate what's failed, what's still hanging or under load, and what's at risk next.

Make-safe first

We stabilize or remove the immediate hazard — hangers, split limbs, or a leaning trunk — before full cleanup.

Removal & cleanup

Controlled removal of the failed tree or limbs, then debris haul-off so you can get back to normal.

Follow-up care

We assess nearby trees for related damage and recommend any pruning or risk work to reduce the next failure.
