01 / Front end

Interfaces built
to spec.

The front end is the only part of a product most people will ever touch. Type, spacing, load time, and keyboard behavior are engineering requirements here, not decoration applied at the end.

  • Design systems and component libraries that survive contact
  • React, Next.js, Astro, or plain HTML when that is sufficient
  • Accessibility to WCAG 2.2 AA, tested by keyboard and screen reader
  • Core Web Vitals budgets, measured on low-end devices
  • Marketing sites, dashboards, and internal tooling
  • Motion used to explain state, never to decorate it

02 / Back end

The parts that
must not fail.

Accounts, permissions, payments, email, webhooks, retries. This is where a product either keeps its promises or begins losing them quietly. We build it to be boring to operate and easy to hand over.

  • REST and GraphQL APIs with versioning that holds
  • Authentication, roles, and permissions defined once
  • Payments, email, and integrations that fail without data loss
  • Background jobs, queues, and scheduled work
  • Node, Go, and Python — selected per project
  • Logging, uptime checks, and alerts that reach a person

03 / Database

Data modeled
to hold.

Most slowdowns and most expensive rewrites begin in the schema. We model data around how the business actually operates, then make sure the second and third years of migrations do not undo it.

  • Schema design and data modeling from workflow, not mockup
  • Reversible migrations, tested against production-sized data
  • Indexing, query tuning, and EXPLAIN plans read line by line
  • Postgres, MySQL, SQLite, and Redis where each belongs
  • Backups, replicas, and recovery drills that have been run
  • Reporting and cleanup for the data no one was watching

04 / AI implementation

Applied under
constraint.

We take AI seriously enough to refuse it when it does not belong. When it does, it runs inside a workflow your team already uses, with an evaluation set, a cost ceiling, and a person accountable for the output.

  • Retrieval over your own documents, contracts, and history
  • Evaluation sets defined before the feature is built
  • Guardrails, logging, and human review where stakes are real
  • Latency and token budgets agreed in dollars
  • Assistants inside the tools staff already open daily
  • Automation of the repetitive, not the judgment work

05 / Consulting

A second read,
in writing.

Sometimes the answer is not another build. We read the codebase, sit in the meeting, and deliver the finding early — with the tradeoffs named and the fixes ordered by consequence.

  • Architecture and code review, prioritized by risk
  • Technical due diligence before you buy, hire, or rebuild
  • Scoping and estimates suitable for a budget meeting
  • Rescue work on stalled projects and half-finished migrations
  • Pair sessions to unblock a team that is close but stuck
  • Vendor and stack selection judged on total cost

Position

Most sites and products are a pile of borrowed parts held together by hope. We build things you can open in five years and still understand.

06 / Method

How the work runs

Four stages. Discovery is not a phase that outlasts the build.

  1. 01

    A conversation

    Thirty minutes. What you are trying to do, what exists now, and whether we are the right studio for it. If we are not, we say so and name someone better.

  2. 02

    A written scope

    What gets built, what does not, what it costs, and when it lands. Agreed in writing before any code exists, including the assumptions that could move the number.

  3. 03

    Build in the open

    A shared repository and a working deployment from the first week. You see progress as it happens, and nothing arrives as a surprise at the end.

  4. 04

    Hand off, or stay

    Documentation, a deploy you can run yourself, and every credential in your name. If you want us to maintain it, the terms assume you might leave.
